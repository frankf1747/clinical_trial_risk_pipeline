# M8: Competing-risks survival model

**Goal.** Predict *when* a trial ends, and how: the probability that it is terminated within t years of its start, with completion as a competing event and still-running trials censored at the snapshot date. For a running trial, the probability that it is terminated within the next 2 years given how long it has already run. Evaluate it with metrics built for censored, competing-risk data, and compare it with v4 on the same trials.

**Why.** v4 is a yes/no model on finished trials only. It drops the ~30,000 running trials, its recent-cohort numbers are biased (terminated trials finish sooner, so resolved 2017–2020 trials over-represent terminations: 18.7% vs 14.8%), and it cannot say when. A survival model uses the running trials as censored observations, is evaluated with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights so the recent cohort counts honestly, and gives a time-specific risk a reader can act on.

## Design

- **Time:** start date to completion date (`RAW_SPONSOR_OUTCOMES.completion_date`) for completed and terminated trials; start date to the AACT snapshot (2026-09-26) for running trials (censored). Labeled trials with no completion date (~3%) are left out and counted. Outcomes live in their own view, `TRIAL_OUTCOMES`, never in `TRIAL_FEATURES`.
- **Model:** discrete-time competing-risks hazards. Each trial becomes one row per 6-month period it was at risk (up to 8 years); a LightGBM multiclass model predicts, per period, {still running, terminated, completed} from the v4 features, the registration text (TF-IDF → SVD, fit on training trials only) and the period index. Cumulative incidence of termination: CIF(t) = Σ S(k−1)·h_term(k), with S the probability of being still running. Conditional risk for a running trial: the same sum from its elapsed period, divided by S at that period.
- **Splits:** as v4. Fit on 2008–2014 starts (labeled, plus running trials that started then, as censored); tune on an inner split (fit < 2013, validate 2013–2014); test on 2015–2016 starts (nearly complete follow-up) and 2017–2020 starts (heavily censored: the honest test of the method).
- **Metrics** (time t = 1, 2, 3, 5 years):
  - time-dependent AUC for termination by t, competing-risk definition (cases: terminated by t; controls: still running at t or completed by t), IPCW (Blanche et al. 2013);
  - IPCW Brier score of the predicted CIF(t), against the Aalen–Johansen null model;
  - calibration: predicted CIF(t) by decile vs Aalen–Johansen observed CIF(t) in each decile;
  - v4's binary score, ranked the same way, as the comparison.
- **Storage:** `models/survival/sN/` (its own registry, so `make score` and the lookup keep using vN until the survival model earns its place).

## Tasks

1. `sql/50_outcomes.sql`: `TRIAL_OUTCOMES` view (nct_id, split, start_date, label, end_date). DuckDB test.
2. `src/ctrisk/ml/survival_eval.py`: Kaplan–Meier of censoring, Aalen–Johansen CIF, IPCW competing-risk time-dependent AUC and Brier, calibration by decile. Tested against hand-computed cases and against plain AUC when nothing is censored.
3. `src/ctrisk/ml/survival.py`: durations, person-period expansion, `SurvivalModel` (fit, hazards, cif, conditional_cif). Tested on synthetic data with a known hazard.
4. `src/ctrisk/ml/train_survival.py`: tune, fit, evaluate on test and recent, compare with v4, save `models/survival/sN/`. `make survival`.
5. Report: survival section in README and model card; then add "chance of termination in the next 2 years" to the lookup if the evaluation supports it.

## Status (2026-10-03): done

- Survival v1 (`models/survival/v1/`): fit on 35,792 trials that started 2008–2014 (4,912 terminated, 30,333 completed, 547 still running and censored; 1,094 without a completion date left out); tuned num_leaves 31, 315 trees.
- Time-AUC for termination by t, 2015–16 starts: 0.671 / 0.646 / 0.661 / 0.683 at 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 years, vs 0.552 / 0.614 / 0.650 / 0.676 for v4; 2-year gain +0.009 to +0.054. On 2017–20 starts (13% still running): 2-year 0.646 vs 0.591, gain +0.041 to +0.071.
- Calibration: understated for later starts (2-year 4.9% predicted vs 5.7% observed for 2015–16) because the risk rose: censoring-adjusted 2-year termination 5.1% (2013–14 starts) → 7.4% (2019–20). Reported, not recalibrated; the app calls the number a lower bound.
- In the lookup: 26,328 running trials carry a next-2-year probability and its rank (not-yet-recruiting trials from zero elapsed time; trials past 6 years left blank). `docs/survival_card.md` generated by `make report`.
- Next: recalibration on the most recent fully observed cohort, and survival out-of-fold scores for finished trials if the lookup should show timing for them too.

## Recalibration (2026-10-04): survival v3

- **Problem.** Trials that started 2015 or later were terminated more often than the hazards (learned from 2008–2014 starts) expected, by calendar year: observed / expected 1.09–1.16 in 2016–2019, 1.18–1.29 in 2020–2024. Completions ran close to expectation (0.86–0.99). The learned termination risks were also too spread out: at a 2023 landmark the safest tenth was predicted 2.2% vs 5.0% observed, the riskiest 16.2% vs 14.8%.
- **Reporting lag.** In calendar 2025–2026, completions fall short too (0.71, 0.63), so sponsors record endings late. That makes recent cohorts look safer than they are (2023–24 starts: 4.7% within 2 years). The last 1.7 years before the snapshot are excluded from anything calibrated.
- **Method** (`ctrisk.ml.recalibrate`). The per-period log-odds of terminating vs running are mapped through an intercept and a slope, and the same for completing, by maximum likelihood on person-periods in calendar [snapshot − 4.7 y, snapshot − 1.7 y), from trials not used to fit the hazards (starts 2015+). Served: terminating −0.98 + 0.66 × learned; completing −0.21 + 0.98 × learned. The lookup's next-2-year risk goes through the same hazards, so it is recalibrated too.
- **Backtest.** At landmarks 2019-01 to 2023-01, running trials (≤ 6 years in) get their next-2-year risk, recalibrated on calendar time ending 1.7 years before the landmark. Observed 8.1–10.3%; raw 7.5–7.7%; recalibrated 7.8–8.4%. Time-AUC 0.583–0.602 vs 0.536–0.562 for the yes/no model. Recalibration closes a third to two-thirds of the gap in the mean and most of the spread, but predictions still trail a rising risk.
- **Reproducibility.** Survival v2 and a first v3 fit differed with identical data and settings, because rows came back from Snowflake in no fixed order (LightGBM row sampling and the text SVD depend on it). Every training read now orders by `nct_id`. Across the three fits, the 2015–16 2-year gain over the yes/no model was +0.02 to +0.03 and its 95% CI crossed zero in two of them; the 2017–20 gain was +0.043 to +0.078 in all three. The README states the former as not significant.
